Workshop Lab Summary: A bent internal pin inside the PlayStation 5 or PS4 HDMI port causes 'No Signal' on TV. Repeated cable wiggling can rip the motherboard micro-pads or blow the Panasonic HDMI encoder IC. Here is our bench restoration process.

📊 PlayStation Video Signal Loss & Motherboard Triage

FAULT 1
Bent Port Pins
Pins 1-19 pushed backward or bridged together inside the HDMI port. Results in TV 'No Signal' / White Light.
➔
FAULT 2
Blown Encoder IC
Bridged HDMI pins send 5V rail into the Panasonic MN864729 / MN864739 video encoder chip, blowing internal filters.
➔
FAULT 3
Blue Light of Death (BLOD)
Pulsing blue light with no white transition = APU solder ball cracking or corrupt Southbridge flash memory.
➔
LAB FIX
Microscope Hot-Air Port Replacement
Microscope hot-air removal of damaged port, trace UV masking, OEM reinforced connector soldering, and liquid metal respread.

Verified Failure Symptoms

Following diagnostics bench tests at our Manpada workshop lab in Thane, here are the most frequently verified failure patterns:

  • Console turns on with white indicator light, but the TV says 'No Signal' or displays flickering static snow.
  • HDMI cable feels loose, wobbly, or has to be held at a specific upward angle to display picture.
  • Visible bent, missing, or twisted gold contact fingers when looking inside the rear HDMI socket.
  • Console turns on with blue light pulsing continuously and never turns solid white (classic BLOD).

Bench-Tested DIY Triage Steps

Before bringing your hardware or equipment into our diagnostic center, perform these verified technician steps:

Crucial Warning: Never Poke Needles Inside the Port!

Attempting to unbend HDMI pins with tweezers or safety pins while the power cable is plugged in shorts the 5V Hot Plug Detect line directly into the HDMI retimer IC, causing ₹5,000+ in extra chip damage!

Step 1: Test Safe Mode Resolution

Turn console completely off. Hold down the Power button on the front panel until you hear two distinct beeps (about 7 seconds). If picture displays in 480p Safe Mode, change resolution manually.

Step 2: Inspect HDMI Cable on Another Device

Test the exact HDMI cable with a set-top box or laptop to rule out faulty cable conductors.
